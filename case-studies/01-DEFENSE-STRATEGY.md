# Case study: from one obvious build to meaningful defense choices

**Focus:** level design, economy and hero participation.  
**Evidence:** historical owner playtests and a dated gameplay package, not a controlled player study.

## Problem

In an early three-wave version, the designer repeatedly chose four archer towers. Limited gold and a four-tower cap left little room to test other defenses. The mission felt easy, but switching the hero between lanes to catch escaping enemies was enjoyable.

The design question was not simply how to make enemies stronger. It was how to retain that useful hero movement while making preparation more interesting.

## Hypothesis and changes

More room for a mixed build and a position covering two approaches could create additional choices: defend each lane separately, concentrate coverage near the junction, or let the hero compensate for a weak side.

The November 1 package documented a six-tower cap, revised wave rewards, changes to the stone tower and a shared defense area. These were historical trial values, not the current balance specification.

## What the playtests revealed

| Reported strategy | Reported result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Four archers in the earlier version | Clear felt too easy; hero lane switching was enjoyable | Preserve active response, expand build choices |
| Mixed towers near the junction; deliberately allow cart destruction | Third wave cleared without hero support | Shared coverage could become too dominant |
| Concentrated defense after towers gained health and could be attacked | Two towers lost in wave two; all defenses lost and defeat in wave three | The position now carried a meaningful risk |
| Reinforce the upper lane; defend the lower lane with the hero, then add lower-lane towers | Mission completed | Distributed investment and hero support formed a viable alternative |

These reports came from different builds. They do not isolate one variable or prove a target win rate. They show why the next experiments changed direction.

## Follow-up decisions

- Add barricades to protect damage-dealing towers and make frontline placement a decision.
- Check road width against a barricade's actual footprint: one early barricade could be bypassed.
- Shorten travel and revisit wave timing after reports of long waits between enemy types.
- Give towers clearer jobs: long-range single-target archers, slow area damage from stone towers, faster chain attacks from lightning towers.
- Defer the slowing tower on the first map when its value was unclear alongside barricades.

## What this demonstrates

I used a successful strategy as evidence, not just a failed attempt. A level that can be completed is not necessarily interesting. I looked for the decisions the player actually made and adjusted both space and systems in response.

## Next controlled comparison

Use the same hero profile and balance preset for a junction defense and a split-lane defense. Record tower losses, damage to objectives, gold by wave and moments requiring hero intervention. Change one balance variable between comparisons. Do not treat an intentionally opened side route as equivalent to a closed one.

Sources: E03, E04, E07, E08. No numeric improvement in engagement is claimed.
